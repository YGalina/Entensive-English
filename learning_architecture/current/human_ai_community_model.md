# Human, AI and Community Learning Model

Date: 2026-07-14

Principle: AI increases practice volume. Humans create accountability, unpredictability, social meaning, and transfer pressure. Native speakers are not automatically teachers.

## 1. AI Layer

### Best Uses

- low-stakes rehearsal;
- role-play;
- immediate prompts;
- transcript capture;
- feedback on recurring patterns;
- retry without shame;
- scenario variation.

### Guardrails

AI tasks must have:

- level;
- communicative goal;
- target chunks;
- feedback timing;
- retry;
- recycling;
- transcript;
- privacy/consent.

### Not Enough

AI conversation alone cannot be sold as proven transfer to human conversation. That remains a product hypothesis.

## 2. Human Coach Layer

Formats:

- weekly 20-minute speaking checkpoint;
- monthly 30-minute diagnostic;
- async voice note feedback;
- small group fluency practice;
- external CEFR/OPI-style sample review.

Coach role:

- diagnose bottleneck;
- choose next target;
- give limited correction;
- force retry;
- validate transfer.

Coach should not:

- lecture grammar for most of the session;
- correct every error;
- let conversation drift without targets.

## 3. Peer Practice

Best after learner has:

- 5 saved outputs;
- repair phrases;
- one role-play;
- one speaking sample.

Format:

1. warm-up phrase;
2. prepared answer;
3. partner follow-up;
4. repair moment;
5. one useful phrase saved.

Peer practice trains:

- turn-taking;
- tolerance of imperfect speech;
- retrieval under mild pressure.

## 4. Native Speaker Participation

### Why They Might Participate

- paid micro-session;
- cultural exchange;
- volunteer community;
- curiosity;
- language exchange;
- local friendship/events;
- status as mentor.

### Risks

- not trained to correct;
- overcorrecting accent;
- giving bad explanations;
- flirting/safety issues;
- ghosting;
- mismatched expectations.

### Required Controls

- role: conversation partner, not teacher unless certified;
- short training;
- correction protocol;
- moderation;
- reporting/safety;
- matching by topic and availability;
- session scripts;
- payment or clear exchange value;
- quality rating based on safety and usefulness, not charm alone.

## 5. Film Clubs

Purpose:

- cultural input;
- opinion language;
- retell;
- discussion moves.

Format:

- watch selected scene/episode before;
- receive 5 phrases;
- discuss with roles;
- moderator captures phrases;
- repeat one opinion after correction.

## 6. Book Clubs

Purpose:

- sustained input;
- interpretation;
- argument;
- vocabulary depth.

Format:

- short assigned fragment;
- 3 phrases;
- one summary;
- one quote;
- one personal connection;
- discussion with follow-up questions.

## 7. Cafe Conversations

Purpose:

- low-stakes real interaction.

Format:

- 4-6 learners;
- one facilitator;
- 45 minutes;
- one theme;
- repair phrase challenge;
- no public correction during vulnerable speech;
- short debrief.

## 8. Language Walks

Purpose:

- embodied, real-world vocabulary and confidence.

Format:

- small group walk;
- describe surroundings;
- ask simple questions;
- record 3 phrases;
- one voice note after.

## 9. Task-Based Events

Examples:

- plan a trip;
- solve a housing problem;
- interview a guest;
- present a book;
- negotiate a schedule.

Success:

- task achieved;
- target functions used;
- repair occurred;
- post-task retry completed.

## 10. Accountability Groups

Purpose:

- habit stability.

Format:

- 4-6 learners;
- weekly plan;
- daily micro-check;
- no shame for missed days;
- recovery protocol.

Metric:

- weekly hours;
- outputs created;
- returns after pause.

## 11. Human Layer Sequence

1. AI rehearsal.
2. Peer micro-practice.
3. Coach checkpoint.
4. Structured club.
5. Native-speaker/light authentic interaction.
6. Real-life mission.

Do not throw an anxious B1 learner directly into unstructured native conversation and call that immersion.
